# water-quality-edta-analysis
An analytical chemistry project investigating water hardness through EDTA complexometric titration.
Water-Quality Analysis Using EDTA Titration

Overview

This project investigates water hardness using EDTA complexometric titration. The technique helps estimate the concentration of hardness-causing metal ions, mainly calcium and magnesium, in a water sample.

Objectives

- Understand the concept of temporary and permanent water hardness.
- Study the principle of complexometric titration.
- Learn how EDTA forms complexes with metal ions.
- Estimate total water hardness using titration measurements.
- Understand the importance of water-quality testing.

Principle

EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) forms stable complexes with calcium and magnesium ions present in hard water. Under suitable experimental conditions, the titration endpoint indicates when the metal ions have reacted with the EDTA.

The measured volume of EDTA can be used to calculate the hardness of the water sample.

Methodology

1. Prepare the water sample according to the laboratory procedure.
2. Fill a burette with the standardized EDTA solution.
3. Transfer a measured volume of the sample into a conical flask.
4. Add the appropriate buffer and indicator as specified by the experiment.
5. Titrate with EDTA until the prescribed endpoint is reached.
6. Record the titration readings.
7. Calculate the water hardness using the appropriate formula.

Note: This is a general method. Use the exact procedure and reagents specified by your laboratory.

Parameters Studied

- EDTA volume used during titration
- Titration endpoint
- Estimated total hardness of the water sample
